# KM:R 42
[[KM-R 41|← Previous: KM:R 41]] · [[KM-R 43|Next: KM:R 43 →]]

- Examining the belongings of the cultists, the team discovers they were wearing an amulet which seemed to be composed of seven figures. Rex searched his databanks, and discovers that these figures represent the seven elder horrors sealed upon Celesia. The jiren determines that these cultists must belong to the Void Singers, worshippers of the elder horrors. This leads the team to wonder how the cultists managed to overcome the fearful aura that permeates the area around the tower. Igvald's blessing, Rex's android nature, and their sheer willpower were the only things preventing the team from succumbing to panic. Zen, via sifting through his harrow deck, is able to determined that this spot is a concentrated spot of outer cosmic energies. He surmises that inscribing the Mark of the Veil upon their bare bodies may protect them from these energies. A misshappened seven point star within a circle, the origins of the Mark of the Veil is unknown, only that the cave-dwelling ancestors of the peoples of Celesia discovered its use to ward themselves against the madness of the elder horrors that invaded long ago during the 2nd Age. Igvald removes one of his gloves, revealing a scarred and mishaped hand, and uncovers a bare spot on his body. He *brands* himself with the Mark of the Veil, and once completed, he suddenly no longer feels that alien pressure on his mind that was there since he came close to the tower. He proceeds to brand Raggi, while Zen casts *arcane mark* upon himself with the Mark.
- Now protected, Team 13 attends to the children. They open one of the cages first, but once free, the four children within it suddenly start shrieking madly. They quickly rush towards the altar, climbing over each other trying to grab the sacrificial knife. Igvald and Rex move to plant himself between the children and the altar. They each grab one child, and Igvald *brands* the Mark of the Veil on his captive. The girl screams in pain, but then her eyes become clear. They then fill with fear and she begins crying, but ceases to fight out of Igvald's grasp. Raggi launches his tiny form into the arms of one of them, knocking him down. The last child manages to get onto the altar and grabs the priestess' knife, pointing it to her throat. Zen intervenes, and grabs her hand and pulls the dagger of it. The girl goes wild, and begins slamming her head down into the altar while trying to scratch out her own throat. Zen quickly casts an *arcane mark* of the Veil on the back of her head. The child ceases her madness, then wails in pain. Meanwhile, the other children still caged are going berserk, grabbing the bars of their cages and shaking them. The one Raggi was sitting on rolls free of the kobold, but Raggi retaliates by implanting a fear of the dagger in his mind. The child is locked in placed by the conflicting compulsions. Rex is forced to smack the child in his arms, knocking her out. He then grabs the grabs the remaining child still standing, allowing Zen to *arcane mark* him as well. The team then proceeds to *arcane mark* and *brand* all the children with the Mark of the Veil, finally ceasing their madness, albeit now replaced with wails of pain and fear.
- Gathering up all the children, fifteen in total, in the cultists' wagons, Team 13 brings them back to Promethea. Before they do so, they discover that the carts were not fit with horse harnesses, but with chains and manacles. It did not take a leap for the team to understand what the cultists used to drive their carts instead of horses. Moreoever, the number of chain sets and cages also indicate that the number of children they still have would not have been enough for all these carts. Not knowing how long the cultists have been squatting Hushglow Tower, but seeing their wall decorations, the implications are disturbing. Raggi goes as far as to manifest *sensitivity to psychic impressions* to confirm their dreaded suspicions. What he sees makes him sick. The cultists did indeed use human labour to draw their vehicles. He then witnesses the head priestess sacrifice child after child upon the altar, then proceed to desecrate their bodies by drawing alien symbols with their blood and innards and body parts. Raagi ceases his vision and proceeds to vomit what few liquids within him. Igvald has to explain it in simpler terms to the more addle-minded Zen. Raggi keeps the children amused with stories and antics, calming them down somewhat. Zen summons phantom steeds to draw the carts, and they return to Promethea. They leave the children at the orphanage, and explain to the matron their tragedy. Zen proceeds to magically set *arcane mark* of the Veil on all walls of the building. They alert Sister Félicité about their condition, and how they were afflicted by the cultists. Prateria's high priestess will work with the nation's magister to properly treat this children so that they may be able to grow up and have a better life.
- As the Argent Rose team resupplies, the Evergreen Company meets with Bekka, their Marshal. Since losing her only team in the field, she has had nothing to do. Considering that Prateria does not have the infrastructure to recruit, train, and manage scout teams for a nation of their size, the Company decides to instead fund Bekka's training so that she will be better skilled at her position. While she does so, Prateria will have time to build the needed infrastructure. They also take the opportunity to fund Sister Félicité's training at the same time.
- Argus reorganizes his followers and has them help accrue the resources to build garrisons for Promethea.
- Rishoi begins work on getting a hospital built in Promethea.
- During her free time, Davona begins working on her most problematic prisoner, the bandit Dante. Despite surrendering at the end of the Battle of the Stag Fort, Dante had been under close watch and his movement restricted. Acteon had advised Davona that the man is utterly selfish and cannot be trusted. With Ayaki's help, Davona has daily meetings with Dante in the form of dinners. The dinners go on for hours as they discuss Dante, his motivations, and what he wants in life. In the beginning, the former bandit is more than happy to take advantage of two lovely ladies offering him free food and company. However, as weeks go by, the discussions become more serious, and pleasantries make way for deeper topics and even arguments. After many weeks of very convincing and sincere talk, Dante has a change of heart. He is still a selfish womanizer, but he has a new perspective on life. That he does not need to be outright malicious to get what he wants, that he does not need to hurt others needlessly. He is no upstanding citizen, but less of a scumbag than before. Although far from ideal, Davona and Ayaki will take what they can get.
