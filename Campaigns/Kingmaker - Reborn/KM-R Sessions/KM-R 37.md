# KM:R 37
[[KM-R 36|← Previous: KM:R 36]] · [[KM-R 38|Next: KM:R 38 →]]

- After the battle with the green hussars, the Evergreen Company makes their way to the Old Beldame. Along the way, they encounter aggressive fairies, dire wolves, and other exotic forest creatures. They seem to be drawn to the Heart of the Forest carried by the Company. At the Old Beldame's hut, the Company confers with the Green Witch about the ritual, and what to expect entering the Old Arborfane. She warns them that the power of the Heart will draw the native inhabitants, and they must wary of them trying to seize it for their own. Thankfully, they are bound by ancient laws, and cannot be taken by force. However, they will impose tests on the Company, and those who fail forfeit the right to carry the Heart. The Old Beldame teaches the Company *The Oldwood Rite*, so that they may pass into the Old Arborfane without the forest being aggressive against them.
- The Company has to enter on foot, leaving their vardo and mounts behind as the beasts are too frightened to enter the forest. Even loyal Thunderhoof refuses to listen to Ayaki's instructions. Ayaki had Thunderhoof round up the other horses and lead them back to the Old Beldame. After stepping into the forest, they perform the *Oldwood Rite*. Taught by the Old Beldame, Jupus casts *feed the forest* to appease the Old Arborfane.
- The Old Arborfane seems to draw the Company into the woods. The paths shift and veer, and they think they hear child-like laughter in the woods. Soon, trees tower overhead like living buttresses in a titanic hall, half formed ﬂoral faces hover at the edge of vision, and the laughter of unseen fey mixes with the almost-words of whispering leaves. Fireﬂies brighten in the gloom, for only occasional shafts of light break through the canopy, and large seeds drift inexplicably in the breezeless air. The atmosphere feels heavy with an unnerving sense of anticipation, and Company has no doubt that they are walking in an ancient and powerful place. Before them is a living arch formed from a pair of intertwining trees. Flanking it are gnarled wooden statues of horsemen, almost swallowed by weeds. Vines curl around their wooden lances, and tiny ﬂowers poke through their beards of moss. Beyond the arch, thick briars and brambles ﬂank a narrow path. The green hussars creek and groan as they wordlessly turn their heads to the Company. One of them stretches out its arm towards them, palm open upwards. The Company notices small trinkets and tokens adorning the green hussars, such as ribbons, colourful stones, small wood craftings, and flowers. Some are new, others are old. Sky ends up crafting small wooden ornaments for each member of the Company to give to the hussars, who accept them and pin them to themselves. They then step back to allow the Company to proceed further into the forest.
- Upon setting foot on the new path, Jupus notices notices a glint of sunlight reflecting on a small object on a high branch. Its source then falls onto the ground into a soft pile of leaves. Jupus thought he saw a flutter of movement in the branches overhead, before a tiny winged figure disappearing amidst the leaves. Retrieving the object, he discovers it to be a hand mirror framed in ornate gold. Taking some precautions, Jupus pocketed the newly discovered item.
- After taking some rest, the Company continues on deeper into the forest. There is a break in the canopy as they find a river running across your path. Dark, muddy water swirls and bubbles around a ford of mossy stepping stones. Taking off this armor, Argus attempts to ford the river by hopping atop the stones. Argus succeeds in crossing a couple of the stones, but the next suddenly shoots up under his foot. Argus manages to keep his balance, but other stones ahead of him also shoot up. Those stones were not stones at all, but the mossy round heads of monstrous humanoids, their long-limbs caked with mud and fish bones. Cethin and Jupus recognize these creatures from old lore they had learned; these were fairy creatures known as *bagienniks*. Bagienniks look like lean, web-toed ogres with wrinkled skin and a bulbous snout located between their beady eyes. They are brutish, degenerate creatures related to banniks (bathhouse spirits). Unlike their more civilized cousins, bagienniks live like brigands in the wild, playing pranks on travelers that can be either harmless or deadly. Their anger is like an avalanche of falling stones: it starts small but rapidly grows in size. Because of their natural healing abilities, bagienniks could enter in a beneficial relationship with their neighbours, but that often leads to the bagienniks overstaying their welcome and abusing the hospitality of their hosts.
	- *“Defilers!” one shouts. “How dare you tread in her sacred place? We demand tribute for this aﬀront!"*
	- *“Tribute!” its companions echo in ever-louder shouts, “TRIBUTE!”*
	- *"What kind of tribute?" Ayaki asks them.*
	- *"You carry a source of green power. Give it now!"*
- Before the Company asks anymore questions, Jupus and Cethin warn them that bagienniks get enraged at certain questions, but a polite and respectful response, apologizing for trespassing and requesting passage appeases them, as speaking a prayer to their divine patron. Sky and Rishoi suspect that their patron is Nimué, the Fairest of Lakes and Rivers. In addition, the Company can also make an offering to them: an animal sacrifice, a gift fit for a noble lady, or meat and beer, to improve their attitude.
- Ayaki steps forward, and formally apologizes to the bagienniks for trespassing. Davona requests that they be granted passage through Nimué's domain. The bagienniks are satisfied by the respectful words, but tell them to continue forward, they must still offer up a tribute. Sky suddenly remembers the mirror they had found earlier. Jupus produces it, and everyone gathers around to frantically examine it if it worthy gift to a fine lady. Sky, remembering his adoptive father's teachings, determines that this hand mirror would fit perfectly in a noble lady's possessions. Jupus then hands it over to the water fairies, and the bagienniks jump and hoot in excitement as they pass the mirror around to each other. "Your gift is accepted, bearers of the Old Arborfane. You may pass unmolested." The bagienniks then sink into the river and disappear, and the river calms down, allowing the Company to easily ford the river. When the whole Company makes it across the river, its waters resume their normal flow. A moment after, there is a splash as something shoots out of the river and lands at the Company's feet. It is an arrow made of a whole mistletoe. Ayaki recovers the arrow, and stores it in her quiver.
- After another period of rest, the Company continues on. A break in the bramble wall reveals a clearing where two shapely feminine forms await. They have skin like polished mahogany but hair and eyes as green as emeralds. They nod at the Company as they approach, charming smiles blunted by something haughty in their storm cloud eyes. Jupus and Sky recognize these creatures as *vila*, fairies closely related to dryads, but much more aggressive, and value oaths and promises above all. They enjoy testing the generosity of travelers, and punish those who prove themselves uncharitable with unluckiness. The glade behind them looks like the perfect campsite although PCs who examine the ground can find countless wolf tracks. It contains a green pavilion tent, beside which is an archery butt and a stone altar overﬂowing with cakes, ribbons, fresh fruits, and ﬂowers.
- The vila beckon the Company towards them. "Peace, and be welcome to our glade," says one of them. "I am Iwonna, and this is my sister Jagoda." They both both towards Jupus. "Peace be with you, companion of the rats." Jupus bows in kind. Iwonna invites the Company to stay, rest, and partake of their food, but Jupus explains that they are in a hurry. However, Iwonna tells them that invitations from the Old Arborfane are not wisely declined. Getting the hint, the Company accepts, though they warn that their mission is vital. Iwonna and Jagoda acknowledge their claim, but tell them that if they are so dedicated, then adhering to the Old Ways of the forest would be in their best interests.
- The meal is pleasant, and the Company shares stories of the world outside of the Old Arborfane with the vila. In a fit of mythic inspiration, Ayaki recounts their adventures through *kabuki*-style theatre, with Arina and Davona narrating. The fairies are fully enraptured by Ayaki's performance. At the end of the tale, they are in awe of the Company, and burst in earnest applause. Satisfied with the company and entertainment, the vila make a proposal.
	- *"You carry a great and terrible burden," Iwona says. "Such power was not meant for mere mortal hands."*
	- *"Why not leave it with us?" Jagoda suggests. "Who better to care for the Heart of the Forest than her own daughters?"*
- Jupus politely declined, stating that the responsibility of returning the Heart was betowed upon them, and it is only right that they see to the task personally.
	- *"Fools," Jagoda hisses when the PCs refuse. "You know not who you cross!"*
	- *However, Iwona holds her hand out before her sister. "No need for violence, Jagoda. At least, not just yet. By ancient laws of the forest, will you let this resolve by a contest of skill and resolve?"*
- Wishing to avoid a direct confrontation, the Company reluctantly agree to participate. The two sisters retrieve their bows and arrows, and propose a contest of two parts. The first will be an archery contest. The second will be that the Company each accepts a slap. The archery contest is won by the first person to reach 100 points. The center is worth 50 points, the inner ring is worth 30 points, and the outer ring is worth 10 points. The Company starts racking up the points, though so do Jagoda and Iwonna. However, the contest is sealed when Ayaki uses the mistletoe arrow. The kitsune would have narrowly missed the centre of the target, but the arrow directed itself straight into the centre. Both vila are shocked to see the mistletoe arrow in their hands. They whisper to each other, though Ayaki manages to hear what they say.
	- *"Is that what I think it is?"*
	- *"It is. Amazingly, the forest must have gifted it to them."*
	- *"It must be a sign..."*
- The contest continues. Ayaki's second shot flies to the target's outer ring, but suddenly, the arrow swerves midair as if pulled, and splits the mistletoe arrow in twain at the centre. Ayaki thus wins the archery contest, and the vila bow towards her.
