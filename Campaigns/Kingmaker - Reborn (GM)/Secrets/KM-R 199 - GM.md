# Notes

In their guise as an alchemist merchant company, the Arina, Sky, Davona, and Argus went to set up their stall at the open foreign market. Orok, not supremely confident in his disguise, remained at the Whimsy Camel. He spent the day researching and practicing skills that may come in handy later on.

The team set up their stall at an empty lot, and began selling their alchemical wares. Sky acted as the head merchant of the group, greeting potential customers and negotiating deals; he had to spend a week beforehand studying how to run a successful business with several of Promethea's skill merchants. Arina sat in the back, crafting certain products on demand. Davona and Argus acted as their guards. The day passed uneventfully. The team did not want to make any disturbances or stand out besides having some well-satisfied customers. The goal was to firmly establish their cover identities.

When evening came, they closed up shop; their stall was now reserved for them for the remainder for their business visit. Arina, Argus, and Davona traveled around the foreign and business districts of Jafarkhet, seeking out information under the guise of innocent inquiries. Meanwhile, Sky made a quick trip back to Promethea via the *doors* because he realised that they needed another magical key for these mythic doors for their team as an emergency exit. He, Louis and Asako took the whole night to create one.

Arina, Argus, and Davona returned to the Whimsy Camel before midnight, and shared what they learned. Arina learned that only three types of foreigners were known to enter the Jafarkhet's Ministry of Amonkhaten. The first were prisoners of the Fists, no surprise there. The second group of foreigners were high-ranking officials representing foreign powers seeking cooperation with the Church of Amonkhaten. And the last group involved foreign worshipers of Amonkhaten who are generally of high status, such as aristocrats or wealth merchants, and are able to provide large, generous gifts to the faith.

Meanwhile, Davona inquired how the Empire's war effort been going. She learned that the bulk of the action was taking place in the southeast region of the Empire. That was its border with its rebellious territories, the city-states that now formed the Free Ashaki League. Much of the Empire's forces were engaged there with the forces of the League. Up in the north, where Jafarkhet was located, the main action seen were an increase of attacks on the Empire's northern border patrols from bandits, mercenaries, and local tribes. This has justified the increased reinforcements of the Jafarkhet's city guard.

The next day, the team continued their cover as alchemy seller. However, around midday, they received an unexpected visit. A formally dressed woman with a badge pinned to her chest, carrying a notebook and flanked by two city guards, introduced herself to the stall as Inspector Bakat of the Jafarkhet's Office of Trade. She demanded to speak with the merchant in charge of this stall, and Sky nervously stepped forward. She told him that this was a routine inspection, and demanded to see his business papers.

While inspecting the team's valid travel papers, she tested Sky with a barrage of questions regarding Jafarkhet's business laws, taxation codes, and so on. Sky had no knowledge of this; he used what he knew about the Empire's general economic policies and what he knew about running a business to give generalized answers. Bakat remarked that while he was mostly correct, it was evident that he was ignorant about Jafarkhet's local peculiarities regarding trade and doing business in the city. She had no doubt that she will find infractions, and issued a 1,000 gp fine to Sky, to be paid by end of day, with the amount increasing for every day missed. Bakat also told him that she will have to log his business as 'suspect', and future inspections will be needed, unless some arrangement was made. Sky got the hint, and Bakat told him to bring the payment for the fine to her or her assistant tonight at the government office, and that she will meet with him tomorrow at the stall to finalize his inspection.

Bakat went on to do further inspections with the other businesses in the open foreign market. The team carefully observed her interactions with the other merchants. They saw that she regularly shook hands with them, and during that interaction, the merchants gifted her with pouches of various sizes, no doubt containing gold coins. The size of the pouches varied based on what goods the businesses dealt with. The more luxurious or expensive, the larger the pouch given to Bakat. When the inspector left, Davona went over to one of the stalls that sold goods in the same range as their alchemy business.

The merchant Namerta, of Namerta's Luxury Dinnerware, welcomed her with a charming smile when she purchased an expensive goblet made of platinum with intricate gold filligree. This made it easier for her to ask Namerta about Bakat's inspection since her business was new to the city. Namerta gave her a sympathetic nod, and helpfully explained that they paid the penalty gift; if Sky had answered all of Bakat's questions correctly, the inspector would have had little cause to issue a fine to him. The 'gift' would have been half of the issued fine. Such gifts helped grease the wheel of trade in Jafarkhet, and gave merchants a wider amount of freedom. However, now that their business was fined, if they wanted to make sure that now log of it was entered and to avoid future inspections, they would likely need to 'gift' the Office of Trade at least five times the fine's amount, on top of the fine itself.

Davona thanked Namerta before returning to the team and sharing what she had learned. Sky determines that it would be worth it paying the bribe; they would not be able to afford future infractions, both for the extra scrutiny they would cause and because their supply of Imperial Ashaki currency was limited. Later that evening, Sky went to the Office of Trade to pay the fine. It was mostly deserted except for Bakat's clerk, and a few other merchants who gave him sympathetic nods; no doubt they, too, were in the same situation as him.

Arina and Davona went out to gather more information. Sky, Argus, and Orok continued to make backup preparations for anything they might try in this operation. Davona learned that the walls and entrances to the Ministry of Amonkhaten were regularly covered in *glyphs of warding* keyed to members of the Amonkaten faith, casted by the clergy. The triggered spells varied depending on the need, changed whenever the clergy refreshed the glyphs. Sometimes they *enthralled* the intruders, sometimes they *held person*. These glyphs were supplementary to more mundane forms of security and surveillance. Davona would need more time to learn what magical defences were set up in the garrison and cathedral within the Ministry's grounds.

Arina learned what type of prisoners the Empire would opt for secret imprisonment instead of just executing them. Typically, that type of prisoner meant that the Empire considered them important or dangerous enough to warrant the amount of secrecy and security of Sand Rock, but also that they have future use for the Empire, such as ransoming them or using their talents for imperial cause. Imprisonment was also favourable over execution if the prisoner had significant power backing them, such as a nation with the resources to cast *true resurrection*. Converting them to undead was possible to counter that possibility, but trying to control such creatures had risks of their own.

It was also possible that the Empire used Sand Rock as their own personal hell for specific enemies. However, Arina learned of another, more dire, possibility. It was possible that such prisoners would be meant to die for the Empire, but their method of execution was not immediate, and needed to be done with time in a secure location, such as extracting any great power out of them. Orok worriedly wondered if that was the fate for Aedre as they carried within them a power the two of them found long ago in some long lost buried ruin. A power that had enabled them to form a many hundred member strong resistance group against the God-Pharoah.

The team pondered that maybe Sky or Davona could be used as bait; have either of them be taken prisoner and be transported to Sand Rock. It would not be easy, but they could come up with some methods to allow the rest of the team to track either of them, or have them contact the team instead. Alternatively, perhaps they could impersonate an already sought-after target for Sand Rock if they did not wish to have their true identities revealed yet.

The team would not form any concrete plans tonight. They knew that more information was needed.
