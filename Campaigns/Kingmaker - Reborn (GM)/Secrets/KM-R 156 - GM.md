# Notes

- The Evergreen Company visits the Old Beldame.
- They summon the Wild Hunt and succeed in capturing the quarry.
- The Evergreen Company refuses to hand over anything to the Wild Hunt. Jupus attempts to kill the white faun, but Rishoi notices and heals the animal. The Company and the Wild Hunt are in a tense stand off.

## Wild Hunt Guidance

- The PCs will need to perform a ritual to summon a guide. Mist rises up from the ground and surrounds the party. Upon completing this ritual, all is quiet for a minute, and then there is the sound hounds baying and the thundering of hooves hitting the ground, growing louder and louder as they approach. The call of a hunting horn thunders in the air. And suddenly, a troupe of a dozen horseriders emerge from the mist, along with a half dozen hounds, but there is something otherworldly about them. The horses and hounds have a feral appearance, breathing out green smoke from mouths and nostrils. The riders appear to be elf-like, but with large deer antlers protruding from their foreheads. Their clothing and gear appear to be made entirely out of wood and animal parts, yet their design surpasses even the most masterfully crafted clothing and weapons of the greatest emperors. One among them, a woman, stands a head taller than the rest, her antlers gilded in gold and aetherite. She strides atop her horse ahead of the group, and her imperious gaze seems to pierce through you all.
- The Wild Hunt Queen forces the PCs to join the Wild Hunt (*"You have sought out the Wild Hunt, therefore no doubt seek a thrilling chase of a worthy quarry!"*). As newcomers to the Hunt, the Queen tasks the PCs to lead the chase. However, they must do so with one restriction. They must accept a temporary curse of unluckiness (disadvantage on all d20 rolls).
- The fawn has a speed of 50 ft, DV & CMD 30. Chase starts at Distant. Each time a PC touches the fawn, the PCs experience a vision in the mist. After the vision, the fawn is Distant again.
	1. *First:* A white faun trepidatiously approaching a fox kit. After a few moments of nervous sniffing of each other, the two begin playfully chasing each other. *Sense Motive:* There is innocence here, and kindness. *Knowledge (nature):* This is most bizarre behaviour, with both young creatures displaying somewhat elevated signs of intelligence, overcoming base instincts.
	1. *Second:* The white faun's herd, led by a majestic deer with golden antlers, are running down a skulk of foxes through a forest, among them the previous kit. The lead deer is about to gore it, but the faun gets in the way, shoving the larger deer, allowing the kit to escape into the forest. *Sense Motive:* There is a sense of thrill, but also a small sense of fear among them. Surprise follows, and then relief. *Knowledge (nature):* This is most bizarre behaviour for a herd species to hunt a predator. Even more bizarre is a child deer aiding the preyed fox.
	1. *Third:* The herd encircles the faun. They stamp their hooves in unison. Despite the faun lowering its head to the lead deer, the lead deer shoves the faun out of the circle, then the herd turns its back to it, and kicks dirt at it when it tries to approach. The herd gallops away, leaving the faun alone in the cold forest. *Sense Motive:* There is a sense of disappointment mixed with anger. Defiance mollified by respect. Sadness, but firm resolve. *Knowledge (nature):* The herd has rejected the child despite its deference to its leader. It is no longer part of the herd, exiled and cursed to be alone in the wild.
- The prey turns out to be a white fawn. Upon being cornered, the fawn whimperly bleats, trembling. The Wild Hunt soon catches up. The Queen congratulates the PCs on a successful hunt, and all that is needed is for them to hand her "the heart". *Sense Motive:* The Queen's wording is peculiar, and her features are oddly steeled in resolve.
