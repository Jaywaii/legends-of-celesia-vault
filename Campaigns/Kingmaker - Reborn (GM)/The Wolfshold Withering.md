# The Wolfshold Withering

Carpathos

# Prelude

The wars over the Viridian Marches for the past millenia have harmed the Thunderhoof tribes, reducing their numbers from over a million to a hundred thousands, split among less than a hundred tribes, and left them with a hostile outlook against two-leggers. In recent years, their latest foe were the merciless bandits that began taking over their territory. Because of these conflicts, the tribes were forced to cross the Viridian Thunder Peaks (north of the Thalamech Uplands) and raid Rochefort lands (in addition to the bandit raids).

Theodore Duloup and his company came down, and battled against the bandits, establishing a foothold in the territory claimed by the bandits from the centaurs (stretch of land southward along the Old Arborfane). Duloup tried to negotiate with the centaurs to cease their raids, but the centaurs just saw him and his company as new invaders. Duloup refused to abandon his new colony, knowing the greater plan of Rochefort needing southern backup for the eventual revolution. With no treaty immediately possible, Duloup was forced to battle the centaurs to expand along the Thunder Peaks to secure Rochefort's southern border.

Grondeloup remains on the defensive against centaur attacks, and Duloup is under pressure from his people to end the threat. However, Duloup doesn't want to harm the centaurs beyond what he has already done. He and his companions are working hard to keep the settlers of Grondeloup prosperous enough while also defending against centaur attacks. Duloup wants to bring the centaurs in as allies, but if no other option reveals itself, he may need to apply more permanent and violent solution. The existence of the cyclops colony to the south adds to his worry.

The cyclopses of Anatolkerat are doing everything they can to get the centaurs to continue their attacks against Grondeloup. Even though they suffer the same centaur raids, they are using their enormous wealth to pay off the tribes to divert their efforts to the north. They also commit false flag atrocities against the centaurs, using magic to pretend to be Grondeloup forces.

# Prologue

Theodore Duloup needs help. All of his companions and soldiers are occupied with protecting Grondeloup from centaur raids, banditry, monsters, and cyclopean machinations.

A new problem has occurred. First, his marshal Giovanni Vinozzi disappeared. The former treasure hunter was searching for a lost treasure sacred to the Thunderhoof centaurs. The idea was that if he could find it, they could use it as a peace offering to cease the centaur raids. The treasure was *Pyrouranos*, a great bow used by High Chief of the Thunderhooves to slay a great evil millenia ago. *Pyrouranos* was passed down from High Chief to High Chief, and became a symbol of authority.

Giovanni gained insight into his research after receiving a dream containing imagery that suddenly made the treasure's trail make sense (Elyssria sent those images to him).

Before Duloup bestows this mission to this band of fresh recruits, he wants to test their skills. He has a few request for martial aid from his citizens that should be simple enough to resolve.

Encounters:

- Contact non-hostile centaur Silverflame tribe, led by Andratreia, and learn what Giovanni learned. Negotiate and perform favour for the tribe, or fight/steal their documents containing clues to the trail of *Pyrouranos*.

## Elyssria and Vorakios

- Within the eastern end of the Jadehorn Peaks lies the mythical Fairy Grave, a planar dungeon, later known as Vorakios' Crypt. It was once a shard of Faerie that passed through the Negative Plane before ensconsing itself into the Jadehorn Peaks in the 2nd Age. It is inhabited by evil fey and undead such as banshees, grimstalkers, and skin stealers. The dungeon's spread was contained by the power of the nearby Arborfane and the then ruling Viridescian Dragons. The dungeon is a font of fairy and undead energies.
- In the Age of Horrors (2nd Age), before the arrival of the Cosmic Horrors, the cyclopian archmage Vorakios discovered the Fairy Grave, and personally led his followers into it. After many losses, the archmage conquered the dungeon and gained his mythic power. However, before he could fully take advantage of his new powers, the Cosmic Horrors arrived. Having become an early adherent of the newly arisen God of Undeath (the Deathless Shroud), Vorakios gained insight on the Horrors' arrival and learned the secrets of lichdom. He sacrificed much of the dungeons magic and his followers to become a lich and used the fairy power to time manipulation to launch himself and his minions forward in time.
- Vorakios and his Crypt emerged millenia later in the 4th Age, amidst the dwarven realm of Barom-gar, and he rained horror upon the region. Only the combined powers of dwarf clerics, elf wizards, and centaur druids stopped him. The ritual sent Vorakios into temporal stasis, and sealed the power of the dungeon. The centaurs pledged to guard the mountain range, and keep watch should Vorakios awaken again, in exchange for the region to be theirs.
- Elyssria needs the power of the Fairy Grave for her plan, due to its connection to Faerie. She has a ritual to both seal and take control of it, but Vorakios has claimed the role of Dungeon Master, and she needs him removed for her ritual to work.
- Over the tens of thousands of years, the Thalamech centaurs' duty has faded into myth and tradition without the clear knowledge of its origin. The magic of the sealing ritual has also weakened. All it would take to break it and wake Vorakios would be a non-centaur to breach the seal. Elyssria found the perfect mark in Giovanni Vinozzi. She sent subtle dreams to the explorer/archeologist that helped inspire him, and steer his search towards finding the Crypt. With subtle misdirection, and without lying, Elyssria and her agents were able to make Giovanni believe the Pyrouranos was at a lost site in the mountains sacred to the Thalamech centaurs, except the site is Vorakios' Crypt.
- Vorakios has not been idle for these tens of millenia. At the expense of increasing his own power, Vorakios cultivated his magical power and art to craft the ultimate tribute to his patron: a field of dark necromantic magic that easily turns all life into undeath. The Deathless Shroud (?). It is not perfect yet. The core of the Fairy Grave is key to this mythic enchantment, and its range is limited. Vorakios has been able to enchant a being as a time bomb with the Shroud's power. He then sends the agent out, usually an undead bird, and it detonates, causing all corpses in a many mile radius to become mindless zombies or skeletons that spread undeath by bite. Vorakios needs at least a week of accumulating power from the corrupted Fairy Grave core to bestow the Shroud on his agent.

- Was seeking a way to bring the Thalamech centaurs to the negotiation table. In his research, he discovered the existence of an artifact called the Pyrouranos, an enchanted bow sacred to the Thalamech. Pyrouranos was the focal tool used in the ritual to seal Vorakios. Unfortunately, the bow was lost in a civil succession war among the tribes in the aftermath of the Fiend Soul War.
- Giovanni has been trying to trace the bow's route before it was lost. He had been making contacts with centaurs from more moderate tribes ().

## Giovanni's Letter

If you are reading this, it means my quest to resolve Grondeloup's conflict with the Thunderhoof centaurs has gone awry. I have kept details of my mission secret, even to my lord (sorry, Ted!) so as to not tip our hand to our enemies. Eddie and I have determined that they are forces actively working against our new nation's prosperity (you know who you are, you one-eyes and money-grubbers). If our enemies caught a sniff as to what I and Grondeloup was up to, they might be able to snatch the prize for themselves. At best, we make no progress in resolving our problems; at worst, they could turn the centaurs firmly against us.

Despite the need for secrecy, I will share what I'm planning here. I can only pray that this letter falls in my lord's hands, or that at least you work for the good of Grondeloup. My goal was to find an ancient bow called 'Pyrouranos'. After a night of particular inspiration, I pieced together that this was an artefact sacred to the Thunderhoof centaurs, a symbol of leadership. Although divided into many tribes, the Thunderhooves all followed one they called the High Herdmaster, the wielder of Pyrouranos. The bow was lost in the aftermath of the Fiend Soul War in a succession war, over five millenia ago. The Thunderhooves have been without a uniting voice ever since. My hope is acquire this artefact, and use it to bring the centaurs to the negotiation table. Perhaps we can use it as a peace offering. Perhaps we can use it to support a leader among them that will talk peace with us.

Through my research, I currently have two leads. The first is the ruins of a Thunderhoof settlement called Galasyna. It was taken over by bandits for decades, but the Wolfpack cleared them out, and we settled Côtedulac there. I believe there to be a stele there with stories about the succession war which may contain a clue as to what happened to the bow.

My other lead is with the centaurs themselves. I have successfully approached several members of the Silvermane tribe, a leading force among the moderate faction. It took a lot of work, expensive gifts (sorry about the budget again, Ted!), and honest communication, but I managed to establish a dialog with them. A group of them regularly gather herbs at a specific spot in the hills (coordinates on the back of this letter), and among them are several elders who keep their history and stories orally alive. They might know something as well, and I think I've gotten close enough to let them tell me.

I have no intention of failing my quest, but should the worse befall me, I pray that you will succeed where I failed.

Good luck.

Giovanni Vinozzi, Marshal of Grondeloup.

# Prologue 2: The Cyclopean Conspiracy

Since the fall of Klorgfang and its subsequent annexation by Prateria, the Carpathosi League have come to regard the new nation as a threatening rival. The city-states came to an agreement that they needed to assert Cyclopean influence in the region, and they collectively sponsored 2 expeditions into southern Viridian March: the colonies of Dytikerat in the west (Drakethorn Glades), and Anatolkerat in the east (Thalamech Uplands).

After the war, most of the Klorgfang troops taken as prisoners settled back to their home towns under Praterian occupation. However, a significant number fled to Carpathos, especially after Prateria razed the temples of Sevrash and Tiamat. These refugees began plotting a movement within the occupied lands to overthrow Praterian rule. The Carpathosi leaders secretly provide support to this movement in order to stall Prateria's growing influence. The movement is also secretly supported by agents of the Viridescia Clan.

Upon the lich Vorakios' awakening by agents of Grondeloup, the lich sent out dreams to the descendents of his former disciples. These descendents had formed the secret Cult of the Undying Eye in Carpathosi society. They had since turned their objectives towards their own self-empowerment, but the oaths they made now bound them to the lich's will now that he is active once more. The Cult has become more active in Anatolkerat, subverting the colony's leadership under their command. Their goal is to aggressively expand Anatolkerat by driving the centaurs and Grondeloup to kill each other so that their corpses can be used by Vorakios to build his armies. They also export many slaves from Carpathos to sacrfice them and raise them as more undead. The leaders of Carpathos have a vague awareness of the Cult's activites, but they align with their own goals, so they are willing to cooperate. It helps that many influential seers and mages are bound to Vorakio's will.

Giovanni's quest eventually led him to pierce through the veil sealing the Crypt, bringing Vorakios' mind out of stasis, but not his physical body.

7. ## Pyrouranos

Crafted by the shamans of the Thalamech centaurs as a weapon to kill Vorakios, it was further enhanced by the Aerlonesti elves to be the key in sealing Vorakios. The centaurs were then tasked with protecting it. Over time, its original importance faded into myth and instead became a symbol of leadership. Pyrouranos was eventually lost during a civil war between the centaur tribes 3000 years ago (~2000 5A).

When the High Chieftain at that time was mysteriously assassinated, multiple tribes fought against each other to claim the bow. All of them were manipulated by the Viridescian dragons in the shadows. The silver dragon Sterlingast intervened by stealing the bow, telling the tribe that it would be returned once the tribes have made peace and all agreed on a means to decide the new high chieftain. However, this never came about thanks to the subtle manipulations of the green dragons. After many millenia, Sterlingast's promise was forgotten.

Pyrouranos is key to piercing the corrupted barrier that surrounds Vorakios' Crypt.

## Charcalthiak & Argentia

Charcalthiak is a female black dragon who slew Argentia (a female silver dragon) for Elyssria. Argentia is the descendent of a line of silver dragons that aided the elves, dwarves, and centaurs in defeating Vorakios. Argentia's bloodline is tied to the ritual that bound Vorakios, and their lair is in the eastern tip of the Jadehorn Peaks to keep watch. Argentia's heart is needed as a component by Elyssria for her ritual to take claim the Viridian Marches.

In her life, Argentia came into conflict with Charcalthiak, and the two became nemesis to each other. Charcalthiak made a faerie bargain with Elyssria to gain advantage over Argentia. Charcalthiak slew Argentia in 97 6A and claimed her demesne for herself. In exchange, Charcalthiak had to give the silver dragon's heart to Elyssria, as well as destroy the bow Pyrouranos that was in Argentia's possession. Without Argentia's vigil, bandit groups arrived in the Thalamech Uplands to prey upon the centaurs, with Charcalthiak's encouragement in exchange for regular tribute. However, it wasn't long before the Viridescian Dragons came and threaten Charcalthiak for intruding on their claimed territory. However, Viridescia offered Charcalthiak the chance to serve her, and she would gain the chance to ascend into a green dragon, become part of the clan, and partake in their wealth. With the alternative being torn apart by the more powerful matriarch, Charcalthiak grudgingly accepted to servitude.

Charcalthiak had a ring of green hair from Elyssria as part of her faerie bargain. However, her submission and choice to join the Viridescians caused it to grey. ## Myles' PCs

- Myles' PCs were once members of the Thunder Companions, heroes united against Vorakios's threat, led by the Thunderhoof centaur champion Althea (female centaur). Other members include:

- The High Beacon of Barom-gar (female dwarf): sacrificed her life (along with many other Lightbringer high priests) to clear the battlefield of Vorakios' deathmist for the final battle.
- The Stellar Sage of Selimbral (male elf): sacrificed his life (along with many other high casters) in creating Pyrouranos.
- Cassivan the Bright Mender (male human): student of the High Beacon and her successor, lover of Althea.

- Before the final battle with Vorakios, Myles' martial and caster, along with other companions (TBD by Myles), volunteered to sneak into Vorakios' lair to find and destroy his soul vessel should Pyrouranos not be the final end of him.
- Myles' companions all died in the Fairy Grave. In order to have their souls be claimed by Vorakios, they and the caster agreed to have the caster host their souls until they can get out. These souls kept the caster alive (the martial already had immortality).
- To survive trapped in Fairy Grave, Myles' PCs had the power (*Slip Into Oblivion*) to make Vorakios forget them for a while, but this cost them their own memories as well.
- ***Slip Into Oblivion*:** This fairy magic was gifted to one of Myles' PCs as a reward for an important service. However, the characters can no longer remember what that service was, nor the fairy who gifted it to them. (3 acts to cast, costs 1 negative mythic or 2 negative lvls, another willing creature can take the negative, max targets equal to caster level).
- To survive Vorakios' *Deathmist*, Myles' PCs and their companions were blessed with a portion of the *Pall of Shining Life* by the High Beacon (before her sacrifice) and Cassivan. This aura prevented the necromantic fog from controlling their minds and shielded them from being grabbed and torn by the undead limbs that emerged from the mist. However, in order to maintain this protective aura, Myles' PCs had to regularly sacrifice their power (i.e. levels) to fuel it over these many millenia.

**Negative Mythic Tier**

For each negative mythic tier a creature has, it takes a cumulative –1 penalty on all ability checks, attack rolls, combat maneuver checks, Combat Maneuver Defense, saving throws, and skill checks. Lose either 1 mythic path ability and 1 mythic feat, or 2 mythic path abilities (your choice). Current and total mythic power is reduced by 2. You are considered 1 mythic tier lower for all mythic tier dependent variables. If your negate mythic tiers equal your total mythic tier, additional negative mythic tiers are instead negative levels.
