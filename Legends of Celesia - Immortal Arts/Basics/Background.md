# Background

# Aspects

Aspect help reinforce and guide how your character would behave in certain situations, as opposed to how you, the player, would act out-of-game. Aspects should not be treated or used as a straightjacket on your character's behavior; they are there to help you be more immersed in the game's story.

The more precise and detailed an aspect is, the easier it is to invoke it and guide your character's behavior.

Goal: what is the goal, why is it important to you, how do you plan on achieving it.

Person, Object, or Faction: what is your history with this person, object, or faction, how do you feel about them, what do you want to accomplish with them.

Ideal: why is this ideal important to you, what do you do to live up to it, what do you do to avoid deviating from it.

Event: what are the details of this event, how did it affect you, what are your feeling about the event, how has it changed your behavior to either prevent it from happening again or vice versa.

## Changing Aspects

Although aspects are key to your character's, well, character, you are not locked into them. Just as how people grow and change, so do you have the chance to change your aspects. Changing your aspects should be discussed with your GM, and ideally reflect the growth and changes your character experiences through playing.

However, if an aspect was not initially well-thought out or is just too detrimental to the enjoyment of the yourself or the group, simply dropping and replacing it is possible with GM's approval. Perhaps your character initially though that aspect was important to them, but adventuring revealed that it simply was not. When replacing the aspect, be careful not to add an aspect that conflicts with your other aspects and how your character has been portrayed so far.

## Karma Points

Karma points are gained by adopting aspects and invoking them.

Karma points can be used to gain benefits.

- Add a 1d6 bonus to one d20 roll. Increase the bonus by 1d6 at 7th level, and every 7 levels after.
- Add a 1d6 bonus to your DV against one attack. Increase the bonus by 1d6 at 7th level, and every 7 levels after.
- Invoke a flashback for one benefit.
- Ignore one compulsion.
- Ignore one non-permanent condition until the end of your turn.
- Reroll a natural 1 from a d20.

You gain one Karma point when the GM compels you on one of your aspects.

GMs are also encouraged to reward players with a karma point when they play their characters according to their aspects. However, GMs are advised to reward aspect-related actions that are significant to the story, and not every little action that are aspect-related. GMs should also not give out too many points per session. GMs should be aware that some players have an easier time to roleplay their characters and may gain more karma points more than other players; GMs should strive to reward karma points to all players as evenly as possible.

## NPCs & Aspects

Because their character and actions are entirely up to the GM, and because of how many of them and how brief they appears, NPCs are not expected to have aspects, and therefore do not gain karma points. This does mean that players have an overall advantage. GMs wishing to add more balance to the NPCs may choose to collect karma points spent by PCs. NPCs may then use them for the same benefits as the PCs.

GMs may choose how long to keep these collected karma points: until the end of the encounter, end of the session, or hold onto them until they are spent. This should be informed to the players and be consistent throughout the campaign.

GMs may of course develop aspects for their NPCs, and grant them karma points to spend as any other PC.
