# Notes

- After succeeding at the archery contest against the vila, the Evergreen Company lined up for the second part of the test: a simple slap of the face by the vila. This is mostly a symbolic gesture, a means of demonstrating their humility in their quest. One by one, each member of the Company receives a slap against their cheek, a stinging, but ultimately harmless blow. However, when Jagoda came to administer her slap to Davona, the caelephilim reeled from what appeared to be a light slap just like all the others. Blood trailed from her lips as her cheek was now darkly bruised. Instead, the glade echoed from a thunderous impact the moment the vila's hand made contact. Both vila recoiled back from Davona in disgust. *"An oathbreaker..."* they hiss. *"An oath to uphold the ideals of the sun god,"* Jagoda says. *"Broken by a thirst for vengeance,"* Iwonna continues. *"You are not worthy of being in this forest,"* Jagoda declares. *"Leave this place!"*
- Arina comes to her companion's defence. *"Davona has had a difficult beginning, and has already suffered for her mistakes. She has since atoned for her failures."* Arina goes on to explain in a heartfelt and sincere manner how Davona has devoted time and effort, and shed blood, to realize how mistaken she was, and how she now helps bandits reform their ways. The vila are moved to tears at the story, and by Arina's delivery. They approach Davona, and forgive her for her oathbreaking. The caelephilim is shedding tears of her own at this point, and they embrace. *"We were wrong,"* Iwonna tells her. *"You truly are worthy of bearing the Heart of the Forest."*
- After the tears have been wiped away, the vila presents the Company several gifts. The first is a vial of ointment that would protect its wearer from annoyances of the forest. The second is a small bundle of incense sticks to help clear the mind. The last is a small satchel of dust that clears the sight. The vila then give each of the Company a chaste kiss on the cheek, and show them the path to continue on. The Company thank the vila, and make they exchange farewells. As they leave, Davona thanks Arina for her kind words.
- It seems that the meal with the vila have completely refreshed the Company. They are able to forge on deeper into the forest. The forest maze ends at a circular clearing. Here sits a ragged old crone, sitting cross-legged on a ﬂat rock surrounded by goblin bones. As the Company arrives, she is cracking open a femur and sucking out the marrow, her mouth revealing a row of monstrously sharp fangs. Sky believes this old crone to be an *ala*, a monstrous creature born from parasitic cysts on treants that drain the treant's soul. The crone is not their natural form, but that of a black cloud crackling with lightning. The Company soon notices that the ala is not alone. Amidst the trees and bushes surrounding them, the Company is able to make out the forms many different creatures such as wolves and fairies, observing them with reflective eyes.
	- *“Greetings would-be Knights of the Arborfane," the old hag declares. "I am Stuhac, the final ordeal of the Path of the Heart. I oﬀer you three challenges: a test of skill, a test of strength, and a test of wits—choose now or the game is over, and I’ll feast on more than bones!”*
- Arina then remembers Old Beldame's advice, that accepting more challenges the Old Arborfane sends against them, the more prestige and success they will garner. Arina reminds the others of this, and they agree.
	- *"Crone, we have heard your challenge, and we accept," Arina says.*
	- *The crone grins. "Which test do you choose?"*
	- *"All of them!" Sky declares.*
	- *The old crone shrieks and leaps into the air. She lands on all fours, and hisses at the Company. "Arrogant whelps! So be it, the more tests, the more you fail, and sweeter your meat will be!"*
- Stuhac produces forth a balalaika, a large stringed instrument. A swarm of wasps begin flying out of it, and crawling over it. The test is that one of the Company must use the balalaika to perform an wondrous song. Remembering the ointment of the vila, Cethin applies it to his body, and confidently picks up the instrument. The wasps calm down and avoid him, and he plays a beautiful, fairy tune. At its end, Stuhac is stunned, and grudgingly accepts his success. *"Well played, fox. You have passed the first test..."*
- Next was the test of strength. *"Zapasník! Come forth!"* the ala shouts. A squat humanoid covered in green hair steps forth from the tree. While most would suspect that this Zapasnik was a dwarf, Sky recognizes the telltale signs that this creature is a *borovoi*, a plant fairy with illusory abilities. The mage informs the party of this, and Stuhac declares that one of the Company must test their strength against Zapasnik. The winner would be the one to pin the other twice out of three attempts. *"Clinch fighting, hurling, slamming, pinning, gouging—torn cartilage and popping joints!"* Stuhac gleefully adds. Argus volunteers to the test. Sky recommends he applies the the dust from the vila to his eyes. Argus initially declines, but Zapasnik then grows as tall as an oak, and just as wide. *Give me the dust,"* Argus says. Argus dusts his one good eye, and suddenly sees that the borovoi has in fact not changed size. Argus then proceeds to easily pin the creature twice in a row. Davona cheers as Stuhac fumes.
- The last test was one of wits. Stuhac produces a gul bara board of lacquered green and black wood and invites one of the Company to defeat her in a game. Sky volunteers, and debates whether or not to use the last of the vila's gifts. His pride in his intellect bids him to forego any such unnecessary aid, but as he is not alone in this endeavour, it is best to stack the deck in his favour. Lighting the incense and breathing in its fumes, Sky is able to realize that the gul bara board has been enchanted to assist its owner: dice roll favorably and pieces shift position. In addition, every time someone removes one of their 12 game pieces during the natural course of a game, the other player feels a stab of pain. In his opening moves, Sky removes most of his pieces, and Stuhac winced in pain from each one. The sylph quickly wins the game, and there is clapping, chirping, and howling cheers from the foresty onlookers. Stuhac snarls and howls, and in a fit of rage, hurls the board into the nearby bushes. *"By the ancient laws, you have won passage into the forest. May you rot in her embrace!"* The ala transforms into a whirlwind of black clouds and lightning, and flies off. Sky retrieves the gameboard, and stores it into his possessions. Before leaving the clearing, Rishoi notices a small, feminine cloaked figure starting intently at them from between the trees. Upon realizing that Rishoi notices her, the figure slips into the shadows as a path opens up towards further into the forest.
- After a confusing time of trudging through the forest, the Company arrives at another clearing. A resounding silence permeates the air. The centre of the clearing is a blasted, glowing crater from which the upper branches of a torn apart tree. The sounds of gnashing and munching can be heard from within the crater. Ayaki senses a pull from the Heart of the Forest towards the pit. Wary, the Company shifts positions to be ready for a fight, but as soon as they move, the gnashing and munching from within the crater ceases. Something leaps out of it, and it is a bizarre sight. An enormous, detached head of a dragon, as tall as a man, flails on the ground, its many-fanged maw snapping. At its neck, a small, stubby dragon's body can be seen. Sky realizes that this is a zmey headling, the living head of a zmey dragon that survived from being severed from its body, and is currently growing a new body. *"Ah, the so-called Knights of the Arborfane,"* the head intones. *"As guardian of the Heart, you must hand it over to me. I shall take good care of it."* The headling licks the slobber from its maw. Rishoi shouts, "Trust not this thing! It speaks only of lies and hunger."
- *"Fools! I would have spared you and let you flee as I feast on the Heart. But now I shall have an appetizer for my meal! I have already devoured my old body and feasted upon the Heart's roots, and grown ever more powerful! And now, let the feast begin!"*

## Tests of the Arborfane

For the second part of the test: they demand to administer a single slap to each character, to remind them of the seriousness of their quest and the seriousness of oaths. This inﬂicts 1d3+1 nonlethal damage, but they also use their *smite* ability—adding 5d6 normal damage if the target has ever broken an oath sworn in the forest. Anyone affected is scorned and reviled by both vila. The party fail this test if they refuse the vilas’ challenge, allow themselves to be overly delayed (participate more than 2 rounds in the archery contest), or if more than one character proves to be an oath-breaker. If the PCs pass the slap, the vila are impressed, and give the PCs a flower: a singing orchid.

The vila are reviled by an oat-breaker, but the PCs can still salvage a success by providing a heartfelt explanation of why they broke their oath (Diplomacy or Bluff DC 30). Upon success, the vila are moved to tears, an forgive the oath-breaker.

**Treasure:** Each vila carries 3 *sand arrows* in addition to their normal equipment, which they use to confound violent PCs and offer as a prize to anyone who can beat them at archery.

### The Test of Talent

The maze ends at a circular clearing. Here sits their final challenge, a fearsome ala named [[Stuhac]], sitting cross-legged on a ﬂat rock surrounded by goblin bones. She’s haughty, scornful, and thoroughly enjoying herself. As the party arrives she’s cracking open a femur and sucking out the marrow:

*“Greetings would-be champions of axe and ﬂame. I am the final ordeal of the Paths of Gamayun. I oﬀer you three challenges: a test of skill, a test of strength, and a test of wits—choose now or the game is over, and I’ll feast on more than bones!”*

**Creatures:** Stuhac sits alone in the clearing but countless eyes peer out from the underbrush: fey, wolves, and others come to observe the final test.

**Development:** Stuhac responds angrily if the PCs recall Old Beldame's advice and demand to try all three tests, but she acquiesces grudgingly if they insist. She also reacts poorly to threats, violence, or attempts to avoid her challenges:

*“Foolish mortals! I am the storm’s wrath and the fury of winter sky. The hill has not yet been cleft to drive the shaft from which the iron to kill me will be mined. Know your place and do not anger me with this foolishness!”*

**The Rules:** Anyone caught cheating gets a blast of Stuhac’s *call lightning*, but she only declares the challenge forfeit if she tires of their antics. To her, “cheating” and “boring” are interchangeable. She’s especially amused by PC tricks that backfire.

**<u>The Test of Skill:</u>** A party member must play a balalaika that’s crawling with wasps: this requires three Disable Device checks (DC 20) to avoid disturbing the wasps, alternating with three Perform (string instruments) (DC 15) checks to play a decent tune. Failing one Disable Device check subjects the PC to the swarm’s distraction ability and means the character automatically blows the next Perform check. Additional failures mean the swarm attacks for 1 round. Stuhac heckles the PC throughout, but if successful with at least one Perform check, she’s satisfied. If they pass all six checks, both Stuhac and the watching fey are impressed.

**<u>The Test of Strength:</u>** At first glance, this seems pretty straightforward: out-wrestle the “dwarf ” Zapasník (he is indeed quite short for a [[borovoi]]). Stuhac says that the contest is to the best of three pins and involves “clinch fighting, hurling, slamming, pinning, gouging—torn cartilage and popping joints!” If anyone complains that this sounds too dangerous, she politely suggests that perhaps a local milkmaid could be persuaded to fight for them, or would they prefer to just shut up and climb into her jaws?

**Development:** Characters who disbelieve the borovoi’s illusory size grapple as normal, but if they’re fooled add +4 (the bonus for Gargantuan size) to Zapasnik’s CMB and CMD.

**<u>The Test of Wits:</u>** Stuhac produces a gul bara board of lacquered green and black wood and invites one of the PCs to defeat her in a game.

Play involves an opposed Intelligence check. For every 2 points that one contestant beats the other, one of the winner’s pieces is removed. The first player to remove all 12 of their pieces is the winner.

A Sleight of Hand check (DC 10) adds 2 to the check result, but this is opposed by the opponent’s Perception. Spells like *prestidigitation*, *suggestion*, and *silent image* might also be used to grant an advantage. Stuhac herself has little skill at the game but is using an enchanted board.

**Development:** If the PC wins, Stuhac angrily hurls the board into the trees. PCs can retrieve and claim it for themselves, but doing this in front of Stuhac leaves her even more annoyed.

Victorious PCs are permitted to depart unmolested. If they beat all three of Stuhac’s challenges, however, she’s forced to grant them a boon.

If the PCs fail all three tests (or just won’t play), Stuhac magnanimously offers to let them go unmolested if someone volunteers to be eaten. If they refuse, she calls down hail and lightning, toying with them for a round or two. The lightning causes a conﬂagration to spring up, which she fans towards the party, forcing them to ﬂee.
